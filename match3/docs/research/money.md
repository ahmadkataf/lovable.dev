# Research: money

_Read from primary sources on 2026-11-13 and re-checked by an independent second pass._

## Summary

All figures below were read on 2026-11-13 from the pages cited.

Country availability is the blocking issue. Syria is absent from Google Play's "Supported locations for developer and merchant registration" table, both for developer registration and for merchant registration. AdMob removed Syria from its sanctions list in August 2025, but I could not confirm that a Syrian payments address can sign up or be paid. The legitimate routes are a real legal residence or real registered company in a merchant-supported country, or a genuine publisher there. A payments profile's country cannot be changed, the bank account must be in the same country, and false account information breaches the Developer Distribution Agreement (termination risk).

How the money flows:
- **Play purchases:** Google collects from the buyer, remits VAT/GST/sales tax itself in most major markets, deducts its fee, and pays around the 15th of the following month. Minimum balance is US$1 for local-currency payouts or US$100 for USD wire.
- **Fees:** 15% on the first US$1M per year. In the EEA/UK/US (since 30 June 2026) and Australia/Japan (since 30 September 2026) this is charged as 10% service fee plus 5% billing fee. Enrolment in the 15% tier is still documented.
- **AdMob:** a separate stream, paid around the 21st once the balance reaches US$100, after identity verification, an address PIN mailed at US$10, and tax information.

New in late 2026:
- A trader-status declaration is mandatory. A personal account that declares as a trader gets its phone number and address shown publicly.
- A Japan payment-collection authorisation is due by 1 December 2026, or payouts are held globally.
- New apps must use Play Billing Library 8 or newer; 9.1.0 is current.
- The classic Google Mobile Ads SDK (25.5.0) is in maintenance mode.

The monetisation design numbers (price ladder, ad placements, ARPDAU) are industry guidance, not rules. The median mobile game earns about US$0.01 per daily active user.

## Facts

- **Play Console registration fee and basic eligibility** — US$25 one-time fee, paid by Mastercard, Visa, Amex, Discover (US only) or Visa Electron (outside US); prepaid cards not accepted. Must be 18+. 'you may be asked for a valid government ID and a credit card, both under your legal name. If this information is determined to be invalid, your registration fee will not be refunded.'  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Personal vs organisation developer accounts** — Both types 'can monetize on Google Play using a payments profile'. Personal is for students, hobbyists and amateurs. Organisation requires a D-U-N-S number (free from Dun & Bradstreet, can take up to 30 days) and is mandatory only for financial, health, VPN and government apps. A match-3 game can use a personal account.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13634885?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Testing requirement for new personal accounts before production** — Personal accounts created after November 13, 2023 'must run a closed test for their app with a minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days', then apply for production access by answering questions about the test, the app and production readiness. Personal accounts must also verify access to an Android device with the Play Console mobile app.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Information required and shown publicly for a personal account** — Required: developer name, legal name, legal address, contact email and phone (verified by one-time password), developer email. 'Google will display your legal name, your country (as per your legal address), and developer email address on Google Play. If you decide to monetize on Google Play then Google will display your full address.' Legal name and address come from the linked Google payments profile and must be verified before publishing.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Is Syria supported for Google Play developer registration?** — No. The table 'Supported locations for developer and merchant registration' lists over 200 locations and Syria does not appear at all (the string 'Syria' is absent from the page HTML). Sudan, South Sudan and Afghanistan are listed as developer-only; Iran and Cuba are also absent.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9306917?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Is Syria supported for Google Play merchant registration (paid apps and in-app purchases)?** — No, Syria is not listed. Nearby locations that support both developer and merchant registration (default currency in brackets): Türkiye (TRY), Lebanon (LBP, paid in USD or EUR), Jordan (USD), Egypt (EGP, paid in USD or EUR), UAE (AED, paid in USD or EUR), Saudi Arabia (SAR, paid in USD or EUR), Palestine (USD), Kuwait (USD), Qatar (USD), Germany (EUR). Iraq supports developer registration but not merchant registration.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9306917?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Payments profile country is fixed and the address must be real** — 'Provide your legal business address as it appears on official documents... We don't allow you to use a PO box address. Later, you'll need to make sure that your bank account is registered in the same country listed in your payments profile.' 'You can't change your business location country'.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7161426?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Accuracy obligations that make misrepresenting location a contract breach** — DDA (effective September 15, 2025) 2.1: You 'will provide and maintain complete and accurate information in the Play Console'. 11.4: 'all information that You provide to Google or users... will be current, true, accurate, supportable and complete.' 10.3: Google may terminate immediately if 'You have breached any provision of this Agreement'. 16.5: You agree to comply with export control and sanctions laws.  
  Source: https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html (verified-primary-source)
- **Play policy on account information and account trading** — You must 'Accurately provide your developer account information, including... Legal name and address... Payment methods... Google payment profile linked to your developer account'. Transferring ownership by unofficial means is prohibited, including 'Buying, leasing, and selling accounts obtained through illegitimate means on third-party marketplaces'.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10788890?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **AdMob country restrictions and Syria** — AdMob is unavailable in Crimea, Cuba, the so-called DNR and LNR, Iran and North Korea. Syria is no longer on the list. The AdMob policy change log says: 'In August 2025, the Understanding AdMob country restrictions page will be updated to reflect the removal of Syria from the US Office of Foreign Asset Control sanctions list.'  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9391084?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **AdMob payment-method coverage for Syria is not documented** — The AdMob wire-transfer page carries country-specific bank instructions for 130 country codes (including IQ, JO, LB, EG) and has no block for SY. The EFT country list (which includes Jordan, Turkey, UAE, Tunisia, Morocco) also has no Syria. Whether a Syrian payments address can sign up and be paid is therefore unconfirmed.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/3372975?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Play service fee in markets not yet on the new structure** — 'Developers enrolled in the 15% service fee tier: 15% for the first $1M (USD) revenue earned by the developer each year; 30% for earnings in excess of $1M'. Subscriptions: 15% regardless of revenue.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **New Play fee structure for buyers in the EEA, UK, US, Australia and Japan** — Rollout June 30, 2026 (EEA, UK, US) and September 30, 2026 (Australia, Japan). First $1M of annual earnings: 10% + 5% billing fee for all transaction types. Above that, standard: subscriptions 10% + 5%; other transactions 20% + 5% for new installs and 25% + 5% for existing installs (or 20% for external web links). In the Play Games Level Up or Apps Experience programs: 15% + 5% new, 20% + 5% existing. The billing fee applies when the user pays through Google Play Billing.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Is enrolment in the 15% tier still needed?** — The enrolment article is still live: you must have a payments profile, create an Account Group (even for a single account) and accept the 15% tier Terms of Service under Play Console > Associated developer accounts. For those enrolling after July 1, 2021, 'the 15% was applied starting from the date when enrollment was completed'. Earnings are counted per calendar year.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10632485?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **2026 fee change announcement and global timeline** — Blog post of 4 March 2026 ('A new era for choice and openness'): fee split into a service fee and a market-specific billing fee (5% in EEA/UK/US); in-app purchase service fee cut to 20% for new installs; subscriptions 10%. Rollout by June 30 (EEA, UK, US), September 30 (Australia), December 31 (Korea, Japan), and 'By September 30, 2027: The updates will reach the rest of the world.' The Help Center now dates Japan at September 30, 2026.  
  Source: https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/a-new-era-for-choice-and-openness.html (verified-primary-source)
- **Play payout schedule and minimum** — Orders processed, refunded or charged back from the 1st to the end of a month 'will get paid out around the 15th of the following month'. Minimum balance: 'Merchants who receive local-currency payouts: US$1; Merchants who receive USD wire-transfer payouts: US$100'. No deposits are initiated on weekends or bank holidays.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/137997?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Play wire-transfer payouts** — Wire countries include Jordan, Lebanon, Egypt, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Libya and Yemen. Paid in USD; 'If your financial institution offers you the ability to open accounts in USD, this option is preferred.' Receiving-bank fees 'may range from US$0 to US$50 or more'. Türkiye is not on the wire list, which implies local-currency (TRY) bank transfer.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2700656?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Play bank account requirements and transfer times** — The bank account must be able to receive EFT or wire transfer, be 'In the same country or region where the merchant account is registered', and be denominated in the country's currency (EFT) or USD (wire). Wire funds take 5 to 7 business days to appear; EFT 2 to 3 business days.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7161440?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Play payment-method and merchant identity verification** — A new payment method is verified by a small deposit challenge or by uploading official bank documents; 'Verification can take up to 5 days'. Separately, 'To receive payments from Google, you must complete the identity verification'; if you do not, 'You won't get future payouts' and Google may close the profile. Documents may include government ID and proof of address.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7159033?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Japan payment-collection authorisation (new, late 2026)** — 'If you sell into or out of Japan, you must complete authorization by December 1st, 2026, or your payments will be held globally.' A second disclosure under Japan's Installment Sales Act applies to any developer selling into Japan: for new developers, 'Your global payouts will be blocked the moment you earn money from a buyer in Japan for the first time if you haven't completed the disclosure.'  
  Source: https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/17595997?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Consumption taxes Google collects and remits itself on Play Billing purchases** — EU: Google charges and remits VAT, 'Even if you're not located in the EU, this VAT treatment still applies.' UK (Google is merchant of record) and Norway: same. US: sales tax in the listed states, including for non-US developers. For developers outside the country, Google also remits in Australia (10% GST), Japan, South Korea (10%), India, Saudi Arabia (15%), UAE (5%), Bahrain, Russia (20%), Türkiye and others. Exceptions where the developer stays responsible include Switzerland and Colombia.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **The service fee is charged on the price net of tax** — DDA 3.4: 'The Service Fee is exclusive of Transaction Taxes'. The tax page computes the fee 'off the net price of the product'. So proceeds are roughly price / (1 + tax rate) x (1 - fee) wherever prices are tax-inclusive.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **US tax form for non-US Play merchants** — 'You must submit the Certificate of Foreign Status form (W-8BEN) to the United States Internal Revenue Service (IRS)', completed inside the payments profile (Play Console > Payments profile > tax info). Year-end form for non-US developers is the 1042-S.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7163598?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **US withholding rules Google applies to non-US payees** — 'only the portion of your revenue earned from US users is subject to US withholding taxes and reporting.' 'If a valid tax form isn't provided, Google may apply backup withholding at 24% or chapter 3 withholding at 30% on applicable payments. This rate may only be reduced if you're a tax resident of a country or region that has an income treaty with the US and you provide a valid tax form with a valid treaty claim.' Forms are refreshed every 3 years. Your actual rates appear under payments profile > Settings > Manage tax info.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/10349995?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **How Google classifies income types for treaty claims (Play vs AdSense/AdMob vs YouTube)** — Payments centre: 'Other Copyright Royalties (such as YouTube Partner Program, Cloud Marketplace - Reseller Model and Play Pass)' versus 'Services or other business income (such as AdSense, but not including AdSense for YouTube)'. The YouTube page lists royalties as 'such as YouTube Partner Program and Google Play'. Chapter 3 withholding on US-viewer revenue is documented specifically for YouTube creators.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **US income tax treaties for likely countries** — On the IRS treaty list (reviewed 03-Jan-2026): Turkey, Egypt, Germany, Morocco and Tunisia appear. Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Iraq, UAE and Saudi Arabia do not, so no treaty reduction is available to residents of those countries.  
  Source: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z (verified-primary-source)
- **US withholding on AdMob earnings** — 'We rarely withhold United States federal tax from AdMob payments. However, we may be required to withhold up to 30%' when forms are missing, incorrect or do not claim exemption. The same page says a foreign publisher with no US Activities (no employees or owned equipment in the US) needs no tax forms. The page is old (it cites 2012), so follow the tax tool in the AdMob account.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/4381375?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Local withholding taxes deducted from Play proceeds in some buyer countries** — Brazil: withholding (IRRF and CIDE) is deducted if you are paid in a non-BRL currency, with an extra 10% for residents of listed tax havens, which include Lebanon and the UAE. Taiwan: 3% unless you hold a Taiwan tax ID. Carrier-billing purchases: up to 20% in Egypt, up to 5% in Kuwait, up to 10% in Sri Lanka.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9384608?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **AdMob payment thresholds (USD accounts)** — Tax information $0; verification $0 (identity) / $10 (address); payment-method selection $10; payment $100; cancellation $10. Thresholds are based on finalised earnings on the payments page, not estimated earnings.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2772208?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **AdMob payment schedule** — Monthly. Earnings are finalised and posted by the 3rd; payment details and hold removals must be done by the 20th; if the balance exceeds the threshold with no holds, payment is issued 'on or around the 21st'. EFT arrives within up to seven business days. Balances under the threshold roll over.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2772140?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **AdMob identity and address (PIN) verification** — Identity: government-issued photo ID (possibly a video selfie); 45 days from the request, after which ads stop. Address: a 6-digit PIN is sent by standard international mail to the payments address once earnings reach the verification threshold, and only after identity verification. It usually takes 3 weeks; you have 4 months to enter it or ads stop; up to 3 replacement PINs can be requested.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2772302?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **AdMob forms of payment** — 'Depending on your payment address, the following forms of payment may be available to you: checks, electronic funds transfer (EFT), wire transfer, and EFT via Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA).' Wire is sent in USD or EUR. Checks exist only where EFT is unavailable. If tax information is required, it must be provided before the 20th to be paid that month.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/2772229?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **AdMob app readiness review** — A new app 'must be reviewed and approved before it can fully serve ads'. The app must be published, listed in a supported store and linked in AdMob. Review typically takes 2 to 3 days, with limited ad serving meanwhile. New accounts stay in 'Getting Ready' until the account is verified by entering payment details. app-ads.txt verification needs a developer website in the Play store listing.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/10564477?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Real price ladders of leading match-3 games (industry reference)** — Royal Match (US App Store): $0.99, $1.99 (Mini Coin Package), $2.99, $5.99, $6.99, $9.99, $19.99 (Queen's Treasure), plus a $9.99 pass. Candy Crush Saga: Extra Moves $0.99, Extra Lives $0.99, gold bar packs $1.99 and $7.99, offers $2.99 and $5.99.  
  Source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/royal-match/id1482155847 (secondary-source)
- **Coins-per-dollar scaling and the price of a continue (industry reference, 2017)** — Homescapes: 'Coins come in bundles ranging from $0.99 for 1,000 to $99.99 for 150,000' (about 1,000 coins per dollar at the bottom, about 1,500 at the top, a 50% bonus). One life costs 900 coins, so the cheapest pack buys exactly one continue.  
  Source: https://www.pocketgamer.biz/how-does-homescapes-monetise/ (secondary-source)
- **Out-of-moves 'fail offers' are a top revenue driver in 2025-26 hybrid-casual puzzles (industry guidance)** — AppMagic, 18 March 2026: fail offers priced $4.99 to $5.99 generate about 14.5% to 33% of in-app purchase revenue in games such as Color Block Jam, Pixel Flow and Screwdom. The $1.99 coin pack captures about 40% of first purchases in Color Block Jam. Currency packs at $1.99 to $19.99 are the main drivers elsewhere.  
  Source: https://appmagic.rocks/blog/fail-offers (secondary-source)
- **Where rewarded ads work best in match-3 (industry guidance, Unity/ironSource, 2021)** — Placements: extra moves after failing (typically 3 to 5), an extra life when lives run out, a pre-level bonus, doubling or tripling the end-of-level reward, daily bonus, free currency in the shop. To protect purchases, give less through ads than through the store ('5 moves for $1.99' in the store versus 'up to 3' by ad) and cap uses per session.  
  Source: https://unity.com/blog/rewarded-video-monetization-lessons-from-meta-match-3-games (secondary-source)
- **Google AdMob's own rewarded-ads guidance (not a rule)** — Users are '20-40% more likely to opt in to rewarded ads versus other ad formats'. 'Well-performing ads should have over 50% of Daily Active Users engaging, and an 80% completion rate.' Rewards 'should not be so valuable that they hurt your in-app purchases'. Put prompts on the home screen and in the shop.  
  Source: https://admob.google.com/home/resources/rewarded-ads-playbook/ (secondary-source)
- **Interstitial grace period and payer exclusion (industry guidance, ironSource, 2021)** — For casual, midcore and hardcore games: 'we recommend waiting at least two weeks before showing interstitial ads to your users'. In hyper-casual tests, a first ad at level 3 to 8 beat level 1. 'serving interstitials to paying users isn't worth the incremental revenue uplift'. Show only at natural breaks and A/B test frequency for about two weeks. No fixed cap is given.  
  Source: https://unity.com/blog/best-practices-for-maximizing-revenue-from-interstitial-ads-in-your-app (secondary-source)
- **AdMob's hard limits and tooling for interstitial frequency** — 'You should place no more than one interstitial ad after every two user actions within your app.' No interstitials on app load or exit, and none immediately after another. Show the ad before the Next or Continue button at a level break and preload it. AdMob frequency capping can be set per app or ad unit; the Help Center's example is 'no more than 2 impressions per 30 minutes'.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201362?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Share of players who pay (industry guidance)** — 'only 1-3% of daily active users engage in IAPs' (Unity, 7 May 2025, citing its 2024 Mobile Growth and Monetization Report). The same article advises introducing rewarded ads before any system-initiated ads.  
  Source: https://unity.com/blog/iap-to-hybrid-monetization (secondary-source)
- **Realistic revenue per daily active user (industry benchmark, October 2026)** — GameAnalytics Benchmarks Hub, global mobile games with at least 10,000 monthly users: ARPDAU 25th percentile $0.0012, median $0.0106, 75th $0.0729, 95th $0.69. Revenue per paying user median $10.93. Day-1 retention median 20.78%, day-7 3.80%, day-30 0.87%. Not broken down by genre.  
  Source: https://www.gameanalytics.com/benchmarks-hub (secondary-source)
- **Typical ARPDAU ranges by game type (directional)** — Hypercasual $0.01 to $0.05; casual (ad-heavy) $0.02 to $0.10; mid-core $0.10 to $0.50. The page calls these 'directional reference points, not hard rules'.  
  Source: https://tenjin.com/glossary/average-revenue-per-daily-active-user-arpdau/ (secondary-source)
- **Typical 'remove ads' price in puzzle games (industry snapshot)** — Listings tracked in 2026 show Remove Ads at $2.99 (word search, block puzzle), $4.99 (wood puzzle, brain puzzle) and $5.99 (mahjong, jigsaw), with ad-removal-plus-coins bundles at $4.99 to $9.99.  
  Source: https://apppricinglab.com/iap/apple/1261300725 (secondary-source)
- **Privacy policy: what Google Play requires** — A link in Play Console and a link or text inside the app. It must cover: developer information and a privacy contact; the types of personal and sensitive data accessed, collected, used and shared, and with whom; secure data handling; retention and deletion policy; and be clearly labelled 'privacy policy'. It must name the developer or the app, and sit on 'an active, publicly accessible and non-geofenced URL (no PDFs)'. Required even if no personal data is accessed.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Privacy policy: what Google's publisher (AdMob) policies add** — Publishers must 'have and abide by a privacy policy that clearly discloses any data collection, sharing, and usage that takes place as a consequence of your use of Google products and services', including identifiers and IP addresses, and that third parties collect information through ad serving. A prominent link to 'How Google uses data when you use our partners' sites or apps' satisfies the Google-specific part.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10437486?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Data the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects (for the Play Data safety form)** — Collected and shared automatically: IP address, user product interactions, diagnostic information, device and account identifiers. Purposes: advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. Encrypted in transit (TLS). The page covers SDK version 25.5.0.  
  Source: https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure (verified-primary-source)
- **Consent requirement for EEA, UK and Switzerland** — AdMob publishers 'are required to use a consent management platform (CMP) that has been certified by Google and integrates with the IAB's Transparency and Consent Framework (TCF) when serving personalized ads' in the EEA and UK (since 16 January 2024) and Switzerland (since 31 July 2024). Traffic without a certified CMP is eligible only for non-personalized or limited ads.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/13554116?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Google's consent SDK (UMP) obligations** — Dependency com.google.android.ump:user-messaging-platform:4.0.0. Create the message in AdMob under Privacy & messaging first. 'Requesting an update to consent information should be called on every app launch.' Check canRequestAds() before requesting ads. If getPrivacyOptionsRequirementStatus() returns REQUIRED, the app must offer a visible entry that calls showPrivacyOptionsForm().  
  Source: https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy (verified-primary-source)
- **US state privacy opt-outs** — AdMob offers restricted data processing (RDP): 'When an RDP signal is present, AdMob will not serve ads that are based on a user's prior behavior'. It can be enabled through a CMP sending Global Privacy Platform signals (Google's own messaging under Privacy & messaging > US states regulations) or per request. In code, the documented switch is the SharedPreferences key gad_rdp set to 1.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9561022?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Target audience declaration and children (COPPA)** — You must declare the target audience in Play Console before publishing. Google may re-assess it if the app includes 'imagery and terminology... that could be considered targeting children'; misrepresentation 'may result in removal or suspension'. If children are a target audience, the Families policy applies: only self-certified ad SDKs, no interest-based ads, no advertising ID from children, and compliance with COPPA and GDPR.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Google Play Ads policy on full-screen ads** — Not allowed: full-screen interstitials 'that show unexpectedly'; 'Ads that appear during game play at the beginning of a level'; interstitials 'not closeable after 15 seconds'; ads triggered by the home button or on exit; repeated interstitials after consecutive user actions ('Made for Ads'). Rewarded ads the user explicitly opts into are exempt from the unexpected-ad rules. Ads must suit the app's content rating.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **AdMob rewarded-ad policies** — Give 'clear, accurate and conspicuous disclosure of the action(s) required and reward(s) offered prior to each instance'. Ads are served only after the user opts in, and declining must not impede normal use. No steering text such as 'watch this ad to support our business'. The promised reward must be delivered. Rewards must be in-app, non-transferable and never cash. Random rewards need their chances disclosed.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/7313578?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Google Play Payments policy for coins and remove-ads** — In-app purchases of 'virtual currencies, extra lives, additional playtime' and 'an ad-free version of an app' must use Google Play's billing system. 'In-app virtual currencies must only be used within the app or game title for which they were purchased.' 'In-app pricing must match the pricing displayed in the user-facing Play billing interface.' Randomised paid items must disclose odds. Apps may not lead users to other payment methods outside enrolled programs.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Customer support obligation for paid items** — DDA 4.7: 'For Your paid Products or in-app transactions, You agree to respond to customer support inquiries within 3 business days, and within 24 hours to any support or Product concerns stated to be urgent by Google.' You must supply valid contact information shown on each app's detail page.  
  Source: https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html (verified-primary-source)
- **EU guidance on in-game virtual currencies (CPC Network, 21 March 2025)** — Seven principles: (1) clear, transparent price indication, showing the real-money price of coins and of items sold for coins; (2) avoid obscuring cost, e.g. several purchasable currencies or chained exchanges; (3) avoid forcing unwanted currency purchases, e.g. bundles mismatching item prices; (4) clear pre-contractual information; (5) respect the 14-day right of withdrawal, including for unused currency; (6) fair, plain contract terms; (7) respect vulnerabilities, with no direct exhortation of children to buy. It applies only to currency bought with real money. It 'does therefore by no means bind the national authorities or the European Commission'.  
  Source: https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/8af13e88-6540-436c-b137-9853e7fe866a_en (verified-primary-source)
- **Refunds are largely handled by Google Play, at the developer's cost** — DDA 3.8: 'You authorize Google to give users refunds in accordance with the Google Play refund policies... and You agree that Google may deduct the amount of those refunds from payments to You.' User policy: within 48 hours Google may refund; after 48 hours the user contacts the developer. In the EEA and UK Google refunds purchases 'that are faulty or don't match their description'.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/15574908?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Developer's own refund responsibilities** — 'you're also responsible for setting your own policies for refunds... You must issue refunds in accordance with your policy.' Full or partial refunds are issued from Play Console > Order management; a partial refund returns the same share of the payout and of the service fee.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2741495?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Trader status declaration (the Play Console requirement tied to EEA/UK consumer law)** — 'all active developer accounts must declare whether they are a trader or non-trader under EEA and UK laws... by February 1, 2027, regardless of where apps are targeted'; new accounts declare at creation. A trader indicator is receiving remuneration from 'paid apps, in-app purchases, ad-sponsored apps'. Traders self-certify legal compliance and give a terms-of-service link per app. 'If you use a personal developer account, you must provide a contact phone number to be displayed on Google Play. Be aware that your address will also be publicly displayed.' Non-traders: consumers are told consumer rights do not apply; false non-trader claims can mean global removal. 'If you don't distribute apps in the EEA or the UK, you can declare as a non-trader.'  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17594752?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **What changes on the listing from March 1, 2027** — For traders, users everywhere see contact details (phone and address for individual accounts), the D-U-N-S number if any, and the terms-of-service link. In the EEA and UK the developer or trader name 'will replace Google in the "Offered by" field', as Google Play moves to an online marketplace model there.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17594752?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Play Billing Library versions and deadlines** — Latest stable billing and billing-ktx: 9.1.0 (released 2026-06-18). New apps and updates could use version 7 only until August 31, 2026, so version 8 or newer is now required; version 8 is accepted until August 31, 2027 and version 9 until August 31, 2028. Version 8.1.0 raised the library's minSdkVersion to 23.  
  Source: https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/deprecation-faq (verified-primary-source)
- **Purchases must be acknowledged or consumed within three days** — Acknowledgement 'must be done within three days so that the purchase isn't automatically refunded and entitlement revoked'. Consumables (coins): consumeAsync() if the app is client-only. Non-consumables (remove ads): acknowledgePurchase(). Acknowledge only in state PURCHASED, never PENDING. Google recommends verifying purchases on a secure backend but documents the client-only path.  
  Source: https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate (verified-primary-source)
- **Ads and consent SDK versions on Google Maven** — com.google.android.gms:play-services-ads 25.5.0 (documented as 'Google Mobile Ads SDK (Legacy)', with the banner 'Google Mobile Ads SDK is in maintenance mode. For the latest updates and features, migrate and set up GMA Next-Gen SDK'). GMA Next-Gen SDK: com.google.android.libraries.ads.mobile.sdk:ads-mobile-sdk 1.6.0. UMP: com.google.android.ump:user-messaging-platform 4.0.0.

## Action items

- STEP 0, BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE: settle the country question. Syria is on neither the developer nor the merchant list for Google Play. Do not use a false address, VPN, borrowed identity or bought or rented account.

Billing (developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate):
- Build the client with BillingClient.newBuilder(context).setListener(listener).enablePendingPurchases(PendingPurchasesParams.newBuilder().enableOneTimeProducts().build()).enableAutoServiceReconnection().build(). The no-argument enablePendingPurchases() was removed in 8.0.0.
- queryProductDetailsAsync now returns a QueryProductDetailsResult with productDetailsList and unfetchedProductList. querySkuDetailsAsync and queryPurchaseHistory were removed in 8.0.0.
- Coins: grant, then consumeAsync(). Remove ads: grant, then acknowledgePurchase(). Do either only when purchaseState is PURCHASED, and within 3 days or Google refunds automatically.
- Call queryPurchasesAsync() on resume to pick up completed pending purchases and to restore remove-ads.
- Show the formatted price from ProductDetails, never a hard-coded price.

Ads and consent (developers.google.com/admob/android):
- Manifest meta-data com.google.android.gms.ads.APPLICATION_ID with the AdMob app ID.
- Order on every launch: requestConsentInfoUpdate(), then loadAndShowConsentFormIfRequired(), then, if canRequestAds(), MobileAds.initialize() on a background thread and load ads. Guard against initialising twice.
- Privacy options: if getPrivacyOptionsRequirementStatus() is REQUIRED, show a button that calls showPrivacyOptionsForm().
- Test rewarded ad unit: ca-app-pub-3940256099942544/5224354917.
- Rewarded: RewardedAd.load(...), then show with an OnUserEarnedRewardListener; grant the reward only in onUserEarnedReward.
- US states: restricted data processing can be forced with the SharedPreferences int gad_rdp = 1.
- The AD_ID permission is merged in from the ads SDK manifest.
