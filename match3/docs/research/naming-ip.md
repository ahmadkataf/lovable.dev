# Research: naming-ip

_Read from primary sources on 2026-11-13 and re-checked by an independent second pass._

## Summary

Re-checked on 2026-11-13; nothing in the findings was refuted, and the recommendation stands with two new caveats.

Recommendation: Play title "Zumurud: Oasis Gem Match" (24 chars), Arabic title "زمرد: جواهر الواحة" (18), launcher label "Zumurud" / "زمرد", applicationId `com.<own-domain>.zumurud` identical to the namespace.

Confirmed: all four Play policies (Impersonation, IP, Spam, Metadata) read as quoted, and the April, July and October 2026 policy announcements do not change them. All nine USPTO records match TSDR today. Every candidate-name Play search reproduced.

New caveats:
- "Oasis" in the tail sits next to two existing puzzle games, "Oasis Magic Gem" and "Oasis Match - 3 Tiles puzzle". The lead word carries the distinctiveness; "Zumurud: Gem Match Puzzle" (25 chars) avoids the overlap.
- Electronic Arts holds two live ZUMA registrations for game software, and Play search for "Zumurud" drifts to Zuma-style games. Never shorten the name to "Zum" or "Zuma", and avoid marble-shooter styling.

Corrections:
- "زهرة الزمرد" is a role-playing game (Emerald Flower), and more non-game "زمرد" apps exist than listed.
- TOON BLAST! is a stylised mark, not a word mark.
- The IP policy bullet about artwork names comic books, cartoons, movies, music videos and television, not games.
- The "com.example is restricted" error has no current official source.

Added: since July 2026 Play requires package-name registration under Android developer verification; it is automatic when the app is created in Play Console, and a name already in use is refused.

Still not done: a by-name search of the trademark registers for ZUMURUD.

## Facts

- **Google Play Impersonation policy (in force 2026-11-13)** — "We don't allow apps that mislead users by impersonating someone else (for example, another developer, company, entity) or another app. Don't imply that your app is related to or authorized by someone that it isn't. Be careful not to use app icons, descriptions, titles, or in-app elements that could mislead users about your app's relationship to someone else or another app." Listed violations: developer names implying a false relationship; icons and titles falsely implying a relationship; "App titles and icons that are so similar to those of existing products or services that users may be misled"; false 'official app' claims; breaches of the Android Brand Guidelines. No effective-date or preview notice on the page.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888374?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Google Play Intellectual Property policy: trademark and copyright** — Trademark infringement is improper or unauthorized use of an identical or similar trademark in a way likely to cause confusion about source; such apps may be suspended. "Modifying copyrighted content may still lead to a violation." Developers may be asked for evidence of rights. "In general, the safest approach is to create something that's original." Listed violations include: cover art for music albums, video games and books; marketing images from movies, television or video games; "Artwork or images from comic books, cartoons, movies, music videos, or television" (corrected wording: this bullet does not name games); reproductions or 'fan art' indistinguishable from the original. With written permission for third-party IP, contact the Google Play team before submitting.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888072?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Google Play Spam policy: Repetitive Content, and Webviews and Affiliate Spam** — Repetitive Content: "We don't allow apps that merely provide the same experience as other apps already on Google Play. Apps should provide value to users through the creation of unique content or services." Listed violation: "Copying content from other apps without adding any original content or value." Webviews rule: no apps whose primary purpose is to drive affiliate traffic to a website or "provide a webview of a website without permission from the website owner or administrator". A bundled, self-authored local game is not a webview of someone else's website; that reading is an inference from the wording, not a statement on the page.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Google Play Metadata policy: title, icon, developer name, description, screenshots** — "Your app title must be 30 characters or less." "Don't use emojis, emoticons, or repeated special characters in these metadata elements." "Avoid ALL CAPS unless it is part of your brand name." No text or image in title, icon or developer name indicating store performance or ranking ('App of the year', '#1', 'Best of Play 20XX', 'Popular'), price or promotion, or Google Play programs ('Editor's choice', 'New'). No misleading icon symbols (fake notification dot, download symbol). No unattributed or anonymous testimonials, no data comparison of apps or brands, no word blocks or keyword lists. "Don't use a celebrity's name or a brand's logo without permission." "Don't use repetitive keywords or deceptive claims like 'best game ever.'" Screenshots and videos must "reflect your app's actual experience".  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Store listing character limits and localisation** — App name 30, short description 80, full description 4,000. "Character limits apply to both full-width and half-width characters." "You can add one localized name per language." Default language at upload is English (United States, en-US). Repetitive or irrelevant keyword usage in the app name, short description or full description can result in app suspension.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859152?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **2026 policy announcements do not change the naming and IP policies** — The announcements of 15 April 2026, 15 July 2026 and 29 October 2026 do not mention Impersonation, Intellectual Property, Spam/Repetitive Content or Metadata. The 29 October 2026 announcement adds a Deceptive Behavior clarification that developers and apps cannot engage in behaviours that manipulate the app review process; developers have at least 30 days from that date to comply with its new and updated policies. The 15 July 2026 announcement states that apps must be registered in Play Console for developer verification.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18100766?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **applicationId rules** — At least two segments (one or more dots); each segment must start with a letter; all characters alphanumeric or underscore [a-zA-Z0-9_]. "Once you publish your app, you should never change the application ID." Recommended: keep the application ID the same as the namespace, and define it explicitly. Caution, verbatim: "If you are using WebView, consider using your package name as a prefix in your application ID. Otherwise, you might encounter problems as described in Issue #37026699." Keeping applicationId identical to the namespace satisfies this. The linked issue could not be read (sign-in required).  
  Source: https://developer.android.com/build/configure-app-module (verified-primary-source)
- **Android developer verification: package-name registration (missed by the original research)** — "Play developers must register their app package names, which may include proving ownership of the private signing keys used for those packages." "For new apps, when you create an app in the Google Play Console, Google Play automatically registers the package name and links it to your account. If another developer is already using that name, the Play Console prompts you to choose a different name." So the applicationId must be unused anywhere on Android, not only on Play, and creating the app in Play Console is what registers it.  
  Source: https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/google-play-console (verified-primary-source)
- **Play Console Requirements now list package-name registration** — Before submitting an app the developer must: provide accurate app information and metadata (3.1); upload a privacy policy and complete the Data safety section (3.2); provide any resources needed for review (3.3); register app package names in Play Console to meet Android developer verification requirements (3.4).  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10788890?hl=en (verified-primary-source)
- **Package-name registration rules for names already seen on Android** — A new package name "that has never been seen on Android" needs only the public key certificate of the signing key. For a name already in use, eligibility goes to the key with over 50% of known installs, otherwise to keys with 50 or more installs, otherwise first come first served; other developers must file a request that "may be rejected". The page gives no dates.  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16761053?hl=en (verified-primary-source)

- Use the brand word "Zumurud" (launcher label "Zumurud", Arabic "زمرد"). Store title "Zumurud: Oasis Gem Match" (24 chars) is acceptable; "Zumurud: Gem Match Puzzle" (25 chars) avoids the overlap with two existing 'Oasis' puzzle games. Pick one before building and use it everywhere.
- Add an Arabic (ar) store listing with the title "زمرد: جواهر الواحة" (18 chars). Do not use the bare word "زمرد" as the store title, since non-game apps already hold it.
- Never shorten the name to "Zum" or "Zuma" in the label, icon text, package name or listing, and do not use marble-shooter, frog or Aztec styling: Electronic Arts holds live ZUMA registrations for game software.
- Set applicationId and namespace to the same explicit value, e.g. `com.<domain-you-control>.zumurud`: lowercase, at least two segments, each starting with a letter, only letters, digits and underscores. If using `io.github.<user>`, replace hyphens and a leading digit in the username, which are not valid.
- Do not use `com.example` or `com.android` prefixes, and put no third-party brand tokens (candy, crush, saga, king, zuma, bejeweled, royal, blast) in the package name.
- Create the app in Play Console before finalising the release so the package name is registered to the account under Android developer verification. If Play Console says the name is in use, choose another before building the release APK, because the applicationId can never change after publishing.
- Keep these words out of the title, descriptions, in-game text, asset filenames and audio: candy, crush, saga, king, soda, jelly, sugar, plus Color Bomb, Lollipop Hammer, Jelly Fish, Bonbon Blitz, Gummy Dragons, Bubblegum Pop, Candy Kingdom, Rainbow Rapids, 'Clear the Jelly', striped or wrapped candy, Tiffi, Mr. Toffee, Yeti, Odus, Bubblegum Troll, and the praise words Sweet, Tasty, Delicious, Divine, Sugar Crush.
- Do not mention any competitor in the listing (no 'like Candy Crush', no 'Royal Match alternative'), and do not use competitor names as keywords.
- Make every asset original: gem art, icon, board frame, level-map layout, fonts, sound effects and music. Do not imitate King's heart-linked logo lettering, candy shapes, saga-map progression screen or deep-voiced praise announcer.
- Give the game original content beyond a bare match-3 board (own theme, own special-piece names, own level goals) so it does not read as 'the same experience' under the Repetitive Content rule.
- Write the listing to the Metadata policy: title 30 characters or fewer, no emoji, no ALL CAPS, no '#1', 'best', 'new', 'free' or price claims in title, icon or developer name; short description 80 characters or fewer; full description 4,000 or fewer with no keyword lists or testimonials; screenshots taken from the real game.
- Choose a developer account name that implies no link to King, Google or any other studio.
- Before publishing, have the developer run a by-name search for ZUMURUD and ZUMURRUD in classes 9 and 41 on the USPTO trademark search, EUIPO/TMview, WIPO Global Brand Database and the Saudi and GCC registers; this could not be done from here.
