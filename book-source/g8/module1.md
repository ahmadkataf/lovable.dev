### Reading (pages 7–8)

Instruction (verbatim): **Read the text and do the tasks below.**

Title: **Schools in the Past, Present and Future**

Full text (verbatim; bold words as in the book):

In the past and about 30 years ago, there were no computers or Internet as we have today. At that period, schools weren't **provided** with technological **innovations** of our time.
The 21st century schools have changed with the development of modern technology and the needs of students. Nowadays, teachers and students use modern technologies.They are using the Internet, computers, laptops, mobiles and a number of other means which are continually being improved and updated. Modern technologies have played a **major** role in education so that teachers no longer have to **rely** on their traditional methods of teaching. That's why the **concept** of a future school comes in. A future school **combines** high-quality education with the possibility of new technologies in order **to convey** useful and **practical** knowledge.
Future educators will have to face the fact that students will need to learn in a **flexible**, **personalized format**.This may mean having a more technology-focused classroom. In these future schools, students will prefer their learning experience to meet their interests, their academic needs and their future careers.

Some of the main features that are expected to be changed in the system of future schools are:
- flying vehicles instead of school buses,
- robotic teachers instead of humans,
- practical work more than books.

(Illustration: a blue flying car.)

#### A- Answer the following questions.
1- How have the 21st century schools changed?
   Answer: They have changed with the development of modern technology and the needs of students; teachers and students now use modern technologies (the Internet, computers, laptops, mobiles and other means).
2- What does 'they' in bold in the text refer to?
   Answer: Teachers and students.
3- Why does a future school combine high-quality education with new technologies?
   Answer: In order to convey useful and practical knowledge.
4- What will classrooms focus on in the future?
   Answer: They will be more technology-focused; learning will be in a flexible, personalized format that meets students' interests, academic needs and future careers.
5- Mention one of the main features that are expected to be changed in the system of future schools.
   Answer (any one): flying vehicles instead of school buses / robotic teachers instead of humans / practical work more than books.

#### B- Circle the correct answer a, b, or c.
1- Before the 21st century, technology was ………… it is used today.
   a- never used as   b- used less than   c- used the same as
   Answer: b- used less than
2- Students will prefer their learning experience to be………… in future schools.
   a- neglected   b- prevented   c- satisfying
   Answer: c- satisfying
3- Nowadays technologies are ………… being improved and updated.
   a- carelessly   b- continuously   c- completely
   Answer: b- continuously
4- Modern technologies have played a ………… in education.
   a- main role   b- minor role   c- useless function
   Answer: a- main role
5- Future education will…………
   a- be based on traditional ways.
   b- depend on functional activities.
   c- rely on books during school work.
   Answer: b- depend on functional activities.

### Reading (pages 12–13)

Title: **A Worrying Time**

(Illustration: a boy with curly hair sitting across a desk from a male counsellor in a yellow shirt.)

Full text (verbatim; bold words as in the book):

What about being back to school? Does thinking of being back to school worry you? Schools are good places for socializing, but everyone worries about things that happen at school from time to time. No single person passed through school without experiencing some problems or events.

The stresses of school life that we are **exposed** to make us sometimes feel unhappy, **depressed**, out of control and have low **self-esteem**. We may feel less motivated in our classrooms or careless about doing homework so that our marks may become low and we don't get good results. Sometimes we find it hard to get up and get ready for school.

Some people may **hesitate** to go to school, or even refuse to go at all.
School problems we may worry about are, for example, finding school work difficult, having problems in classrooms especially if others are noisy, not getting on with teachers, problems at home, and **bullies** who often cause **complicated** problems, etc.
In this situation, the most important thing is to recognize that there's a problem to be solved and ask for help as soon as possible. We may ask our parents at home, or teachers and social workers in schools. They can help in solving the problems that we face.

3- Think of a problem or an event that happened to you during your school life.
   Answer: Personal / open answer.
4- How did you solve it?
   Answer: Personal / open answer (e.g. by recognizing the problem and asking parents, teachers or social workers for help).

## Focus on Physics (page 17)

Pre-questions (verbatim):

Text (verbatim):
**Newton's laws of motion relate an object's motion to the forces acting on it.**

**The 1st law:** An object will not change its motion unless a force acts on it.
**The 2nd law:** The acceleration of an object depends on two things, force and mass. (force = mass x acceleration)
**The 3rd law:** For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Task (verbatim): **- Match column A with B.**

Column A: Newton's first law / Newton's second law / Newton's third law
Column B (pictures):
1. Two balls with arrows of different sizes, caption "The more force... The more acceleration."
2. A person jumping off a boat, labelled "ACTION" (arrow left) and "REACTION" (arrow right).
3. A Newton's cradle (pendulum balls, one red ball pulled aside).
4. Caption "same force" – "small mass: large acceleration" (a small cart) / "large mass: small acceleration" (a car), "Force =mass×acceleration".

Footnote (verbatim): Acceleration means: the rate at which something goes faster

Answers: Newton's first law → picture 3 (Newton's cradle / balls at rest until a force acts); Newton's second law → picture 1 and picture 4 (force = mass × acceleration); Newton's third law → picture 2 (action / reaction).
