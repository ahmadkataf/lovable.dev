#### Dream it, Believe it, Achieve it

Natalie du Toit, the South African swimmer, was only 17 when she lost her leg in a road accident. She was one of the promising young swimmers. Her leg had to be **amputated** at the knee. Everybody thought she would never be able to swim in a **competitive** way again.

### Grammar (page 68)

**Using "Wish"**

**Read the following paragraph and do the tasks below.**

There are many things I wish were different in my family. My father arrives home late every day. He works for a long time. My mother spends too much time using her mobile phone. My elder brother is a heavy smoker.

Rule (implied by the exercises; the book gives no separate rule box): We use *wish* + past simple (were / didn't / -ed) to talk about a present situation we would like to be different. We use *wish* + *would* for things we want other people to do, and *wish* + *could* for ability.

**1- Decide which sentence in the text above is suitable for the following "wish sentences".**
1- I wish my father arrived earlier..................................................................
   - Answer: My father arrives home late every day.
2- I wish my mother didn't spend so much time on the mobile phone........................
   - Answer: My mother spends too much time using her mobile phone.
3- I wish my brother weren't a smoker...........................................................
   - Answer: My elder brother is a heavy smoker.

**2- Look at the following sentences. Choose the right verb in brackets.**
1- She wishes she (were - is) married. (She isn't married now).
   - Answer: were
2- I wish you (will - would) tell us the truth .(but you're not telling the truth now).
   - Answer: would
3- We wish we (could - can) help you. (but we can't).
   - Answer: could
4- I wish I (listen - listened) to you. (but I don't).
   - Answer: listened

#### How to Find out your own Dreams

Some people believe that "imagination" is the best friend in discovering their dreams. But why are other people who have a good life, skills, education, and a good job unable to achieve their dreams? The answer is simple. They don't know what they want in their life. At best, they will be helping others in achieving their dreams. Having no big dreams may be a result of having endless excuses.

Jessica De Bry says: "**Passion** is your **compelling** emotion behind your dreams.Your feelings drive your passion. Purpose is the 'WHY' behind it all.
Purpose is the deep reason for your existence".
The power of dreams lies in your **investment** in achieving that dream, and in having a a strong reason "why you want it".
The stronger the reason is, the stronger the chance to achieve your dream will be.
A dream without passion means to **quit** when things get **tough**.

Some people balance well between work, family and friends: "Work-life balance". They live their life, but they **lose the track** of their childhood and school dreams.
If passionate people fail, they are convinced that they are one step closer to fulfilling their dreams. People who have passion for their dreams will go after them regardless of the **challenges** and obstacles they face. Their passion will keep them focused and on track.

- **to compel**: to make you pay attention to something because it is interesting and exciting
- **investment**: the act of giving time or effort to a task to make it successful
- **quit**: to stop doing something
- **tough**: difficult
- **lose the track**: fail to know what is happening to you
- **challenge**: a difficult task that tests someone's ability

**A- Read the following dialogue and do the tasks below.**

Rule (implied; no separate rule box in the book): A positive statement takes a negative tag (It's a lovely day, isn't it?); a negative statement takes a positive tag (They don't like..., do they?). The tag uses the same auxiliary/modal as the statement (be, have, do, will, should...).

#### Chemical Symbols

Elements are represented by chemical symbols. Chemical symbols are a short way of representing the elements. Each symbol consists of one or two letters, usually taken from the element's name. The symbol for the element oxygen is O. The symbol for hydrogen is H and for carbon is C. The symbol for aluminum is Al and for chlorine is Cl. You should note that when two letters are used in a symbol, the first letter is always capitalized but the second letter is never capitalized. Two letters are needed for an element's symbol when the first letter of that element's name has already been used, as the symbol for carbon is C, for calcium is Ca, and for copper is Cu. Scientists often use the Latin name of an element to create its symbol. The symbol for gold is Au. The Latin name for gold is aurum. The symbol for silver is Ag, from the Latin word argentum. The Latin word for iron is ferrum. So the symbol for this element is Fe.
